Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Irving

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Empty the Drain PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Square footage of ceiling, wall and flooring that has to be replaced.
  • Where the unit sits: attic, closet or garage.
  • Whether the wood platform under a closet unit has to be rebuilt.
  • How many days the condensate dripped before the system was shut off.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
